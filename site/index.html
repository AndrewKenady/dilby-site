Privacy

It's all yours. That's why you can trust it.

Trust Dilby with your family's data and your organization's, because none of it goes anywhere. Your chats, recordings and memories live on your computer, and nobody can collect, analyze or sell what never reaches them.

Nothing leaves the building

The model, the speech recognition and the voices all run on your own hardware. No cloud inference, and no account with us.

llama.cpp · whisper.cpp · Piper

We can't see it either

Dilby sends us no logs, no usage data and no crash reports. We don't run a server between you and your data, so there's nothing for us to intercept.

no telemetry · no tracking

The web is opt-in

Dilby works fully offline. If the host allows the web, it's switched on chat by chat, and only the searches and pages Dilby looks up go out, through a search service the host picks.

per-server switch · per-chat toggle

You decide who's in

The host approves every account, chooses whether Dilby is open to this computer, the local network or the internet, and can see and revoke every key.

host-approved accounts · 2FA

How it works

Up and running in an afternoon.

  1. Install it on one computer

    Run the Windows app on a PC with a capable graphics card, and tick the extras you want. Dilby downloads everything once; after that it needs no internet at all.

  2. Invite your people

    Flip one switch and every phone, tablet and laptop on your network can use Dilby in a browser. You approve each person who joins.

  3. Let it learn together

    Everyday facts one person mentions help everyone else, credited to whoever said them. Health, money and anything personal stay with their author.

Questions

FAQ

Does anything I say reach OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta or Google?

No. Dilby's model, speech recognition and voices run on your computer, so your chats, recordings and notes stay there. We don't collect, analyze or sell anything, because nothing is sent to us: there's no telemetry and no Dilby server in the middle. If the host allows web search, a chat has to switch it on, and only the searches and pages Dilby looks up go out.

How does it compare with ChatGPT or Claude?

You get the same kinds of features: chat, a thinking mode, voice, meeting notes and a coding agent. The intelligence comes from an open-weight model (Qwen3.6 35B-A3B) running on your graphics card. It's strong at everyday writing, questions, summaries and code, but the biggest cloud models are still ahead on the hardest problems. In exchange you get privacy, a fixed cost and a memory your group shares.

Does it need the internet?

Only to download things during setup: the model (about 23 GB) and any extras you pick, such as voice (about 650 MB), Wikipedia (7 to 127 GB), the world map (0.6 to 3.8 GB) or maps and directions for your area (a few GB for a US state). After that Dilby runs with the network cable unplugged, voice, meeting notes and directions included. Offline install bundles for air-gapped sites are planned.

What hardware do I need?

A Windows 10 or 11 PC with an NVIDIA graphics card with 12 GB of memory (AMD and Intel cards work through Vulkan, more slowly), 32 GB of RAM and about 25 GB of free disk, plus room for any extras. On a 12 GB laptop GPU it writes about 35 tokens (roughly 25 words) a second. Voice and notes run on the processor. Linux and macOS work from source.

Is it free?

Yes, for personal and community use, with every feature. Other organizations will pay for team controls, sign-in, scale and support; pricing isn't set yet.

Who can read my meeting notes?

Only you. Other members can't open them, neither can the host in the app, and Dilby never turns them into shared memories. The audio is transcribed on Dilby's computer and isn't kept; only the text is saved, until you delete it.

What gets shared between people?

Short facts Dilby learns, like what's running low in the pantry or when the hall is booked, credited to whoever said them. Never whole chats or meeting notes, and never health, money, relationships or things about other people. You can see and forget everything Dilby knows from you, and private chats share nothing.

How is it different from Ollama or Open WebUI?

Those are great tools for one person. Dilby is built for a group: one install serves everyone on your network, the host approves who joins, and Dilby keeps a shared memory with authorship. Voice conversations and meeting notes come built in.
